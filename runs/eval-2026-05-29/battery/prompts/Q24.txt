Implement `word_wrap(text, width)` in solution.py. It greedily wraps text into lines no longer than `width` characters, breaking only between words — for example word_wrap("the quick brown fox", 9) gives "the quick\nbrown fox". A word that's longer than `width` still goes on its own line (never split mid-word). Words are separated by whitespace and lines are joined with newlines. It should handle whatever whitespace the input has. The existing test shows the basic case.
